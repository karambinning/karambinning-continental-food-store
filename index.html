A note from the shop

A continental food store is a small thing with a long memory. It is the rye loaf you can't get from the supermarket, the smoked sausage your grandmother bought, the jar of pickled peppers that tastes the way it did at home. We carry the staples you'd find from Warsaw to Bucharest to Sofia — kept on a single shelf, on a single street, in Luton.

Nothing fancy. No catalogue, no clever branding. Just the things people from across Europe have been buying here, week in and week out, since before the chains arrived on Chapel Street.

Where the shelves come from

Seven kitchens, one corner shop.

The store carries staples from a handful of European traditions. The mix shifts with what the suppliers send each week.

Polish

01 / PL

Kabanos, rye bread, twaróg, pickled cucumbers and the dry-goods aisle that keeps a Polish kitchen running.

Romanian

02 / RO

Brânză cheeses, mici-style sausages, pickled vegetables, sunflower oils and pantry basics from the Black Sea coast.

Bulgarian

03 / BG

Lutenitsa, sirene, banitsa pastry, mountain honey and the deep-amber preserves of the Balkans.

Lithuanian

04 / LT

Dark sourdoughs, cold-smoked sausage, curd cheeses and the bitter coffee favoured around Vilnius.

Hungarian

05 / HU

Paprika in three heats, salami, sour cherry preserves and the slow-burn pantry of central Hungary.

Turkish

06 / TR

Halva, sesame sweets, Turkish delight, pulses, olives and the loose teas that travel well to a Luton kitchen.

Albanian & Slovak

07 / AL · SK

Smaller corners of the shop — cured meats, sheep cheeses and pantry items requested by regular customers.
